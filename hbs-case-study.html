01 — The Company

For more than 40 years, HBS Systems has grown into the leading provider of Equipment Dealership Management Systems. Based in Richardson, Texas, and backed by over 500 combined years of industry experience, HBS is trusted by more than 1,500 equipment dealerships and rental companies across North America — many of them for the life of their business.

Its platform runs the heart of a dealership: accounting and financials, parts inventory, units, service and warranty, rental, and sales — across single stores and large multi-location dealer groups alike. For the agricultural and heavy-equipment dealers HBS serves, that software is the operational backbone of the business.

02 — The Opportunity

A Consolidating Industry Races Toward AI

The equipment-dealership world is changing fast. Dealer groups are consolidating through steady mergers and acquisitions, while the manufacturers behind them evolve into vertically integrated technology platforms. The dealer-management-software market itself is growing at roughly 8% a year and is forecast to approach $9 billion in 2026.1

At the same time, AI has moved from buzzword to buying criterion. Dealers now arrive with specific use cases, and competing DMS vendors have begun launching AI-powered platforms and purpose-built AI agents.1

HBS saw the moment clearly: to extend four decades of leadership into the next era, its platform needed to be rebuilt on a modern foundation — one ready for the cloud and, above all, ready for AI. That is the mission behind Nova, the next-generation platform succeeding NetView ĒCO.

But modernization at this scale is not something to attempt alone. HBS needed a partner with the engineering depth to build Nova without putting 1,500+ dealerships at risk — and to prove it before betting the business on it.

Proof came early. Nova's hardest technical risk — some customer servers run on just 2 GB of memory — was solved in testing, running thousands of APIs on a small fraction of what the old model would demand. (Page 4.)

07 — The Breakthrough

An Early, Decisive Engineering Win

The clearest measure of a modernization partner isn't the process — it's the results. Nova's first big result came early, against its hardest technical risk.

That risk was hiding in the hardware. The legacy platform spans 400+ applications, and some customer servers run with as little as 2 GB of memory. Naively modernizing every app to run its own service could have overrun that limit — a potentially catastrophic result for live dealerships.

Monza engineered the way through. In testing, the new approach runs and supports thousands of APIs simultaneously on a small fraction of the memory the old model would demand — turning a project-threatening constraint into a validated path forward. It is exactly the kind of early, concrete win that proves the foundation is sound before the heavy build begins.

08 — How It Works

The Discipline Behind the Result

Wins like that aren't luck. They come from a way of working designed to find risk early and engineer it out — across a blended in-house, Monza, and nearshore team running many workstreams at once.

Discovery before building, through continuous "deep dives"

Structured deep dives run across nearly every workstream — plus AI — so decisions are grounded in evidence, not assumption, and performance risks like the memory constraint surface before code is committed, not after.

Program management across many fronts

With many workstreams and mini-teams running at once, Monza provides the project management and architecture leadership that keeps parallel work coherent — standardizing code patterns, benchmarking old against new, and sequencing dependencies so the pieces fit together.

A cadence built for alignment

The teams run two daily scrums — one internal to Monza, one shared with HBS — plus a weekly cadence meeting joining the whole of HBS leadership with Monza's management and key engineers, so decisions move quickly and no workstream drifts.
