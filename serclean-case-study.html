01 — The Company

SerClean provides water extraction, mold removal, fire restoration, and disaster-recovery services to homeowners and businesses. What began in Savannah, Georgia grew into one of the Southeast's largest independently-owned restoration firms — today serving 22 areas across the region.

Restoration isn't a scheduled business. When a storm, flood, or fire hits, SerClean mobilizes crews, trucks, and specialized equipment on short notice — often around the clock. And because most work is reimbursed through insurance claims, every job must be documented to the standard an insurer will pay against. That combination — emergency dispatch plus claim-grade proof of work — is the reality the software had to serve.

02 — The Challenge

Growth Created a Tangle of 15 Systems

Fast growth multiplies the back-office work that keeps the front office running — scheduling, inventory, IT, accounting, HR, and partner integrations. Handled well, these functions power growth. Handled badly, they quietly cap it.

SerClean started simple: email chains and single-purpose tools for communication, inventory, contracts, and invoicing. It worked — until it didn't. As job volume climbed across multiple states, the threads buckled. Too many trucks, too much equipment, too many simultaneous jobs to track by hand. SerClean started losing money to misplaced equipment and unbilled work.

The patch was more tools, not fewer: Dropbox, Google Sheets, and a dozen more point solutions — each reasonable alone, none talking to the others. At its peak the business ran on roughly 15 disconnected systems, and the in-house IT team couldn't make them act as one.

The off-the-shelf suites didn't fit either. Platforms like ServiceTitan are built around pre-booked appointments — good for plumbing, HVAC, and pool services that know their week in advance. Restoration is the opposite: emergency-driven, equipment-heavy, and billed backwards — paid later, through insurance claims, only once the work is proven.

03 — The Partner

Choosing Monza Cloud

SerClean didn't build software. To create a platform tailored to restoration, founder Michael Mock set firm criteria for a development partner:

Long-term value — an investment that cuts overhead and travels to new locations, not another disposable tool.

Robustness — in an emergency business, downtime costs revenue and reputation.

Direct communication — a partner he could talk to and meet in person, not an offshore team.

A fit for a non-technical company — a solution for a business that doesn't run its own IT.

After talking with owners in similar industries and vetting the entire marketplace for any commercial offerings, Michael chose Monza Cloud — an Atlanta-based, AI and cloud-centered firm capable of building him the customization his business needed.

Why "Restoration Desk"?

Named deliberately, after a term the industry already knows — a statement that the category finally had software of its own. To this day there's no off-the-shelf product built for restoration the way Restoration Desk is. The platforms it's compared to — ServiceTitan, Dash — are built for other trades and ill-serve an industry where billing runs backwards through insurance claims.

05 — What Monza Engineered

Purpose-Built to Remove Work, Not Add Features

Monza didn't hand SerClean a generic toolkit. Every capability was engineered to attack a specific business cost — lost equipment, unbilled work, manual re-entry, or the staffing creep that was capping growth. The result is a platform the restoration industry has never had off the shelf. Two capabilities show the pattern.

Boards Outcome: faster decisions, less rework

Monza built role-based "boards" so every department — management, billing, field ops, equipment — sees exactly the data it needs, live. The business impact: sharply higher day-to-day productivity and fewer things falling through the cracks.

The Job Board. One of many role-based views Monza engineered — live job, field, and billing status at a glance — so each team runs its part of the business from a single screen instead of chasing updates across tools.

Trays Outcome: zero re-entry, instant context

Linked "tray" windows surface every facet of a job — appointments, equipment, billing, photo documentation, reports — and stay in sync with the boards. Selecting a job instantly deep-dives it, eliminating the re-navigation and duplicate data entry that used to drain staff hours.

The business impact

Before Monza, SerClean needed roughly one office worker for every two service vans — overhead that rose with every job won. By consolidating 15 disconnected tools into one engineered system, Monza broke that link between growth and headcount: SerClean later absorbed a 30% rise in job volume with no matching rise in back-office staff. That is the difference between software that stores data and software that removes work.

06 — The Results

A Platform Proven Over Years

With the platform in place, SerClean grew weekly job volume from roughly 250 to ~325 — a 30% increase — through years that punished the wider restoration trade, where operators named skilled-labor shortages, rising supply costs, and inflation as their top challenges and turnover ran near 28%.1 The software didn't win the work, but it removed the back-office ceiling that would have made absorbing that growth impossible.

More scalable

The platform let SerClean absorb the full 30% rise in job volume without a matching rise in back-office staff — breaking the old pattern where every few service vans demanded another office hire.

More resilient

Cloud-hosted on Azure, Restoration Desk isn't exposed to local outages, and Monza Cloud monitors and resolves issues remotely — so SerClean stays available exactly when disaster strikes.

Leaner back office

The 15-system tangle is effectively gone — anything that survives is used in trace amounts and no longer central to running the business.

07 — The Validation

Acquired, Retained, and Built to Scale

An independent verdict on what Monza built came when SerClean was acquired by High Ground and brought into its national family of restoration brands. Software built for one company doesn't always survive that transition — much of it gets ripped out and replaced with the acquirer's standards. Restoration Desk did the opposite: it was valued enough to be kept, invested in, and still shipping new capabilities under its new owner.

In fact, the platform was recognized as an asset in its own right — valuable enough that High Ground acquired ownership of the software specifically, separate from the SerClean purchase. In an industry with no purpose-built alternative, a single platform that could one day run multiple brands is a tremendous asset, and the door is open to a shared advantage across the group. For a prospective client, the lesson isn't about one acquisition — it's that custom software built the right way becomes a durable, transferable business asset.
